## **1\. Introduction**

This document defines the **APTITUDE Trust Artifact Status Profile** for interoperable status establishment and revocation checking across APTITUDE pilots.

It specifies a baseline, cross-pilot profile for the validation of trust artifacts aligned with the [APTITUDE Trust Specifications](https://aptitude-consortium.github.io/wp2-trust-specifications/latest/), the [references](https://aptitude-consortium.github.io/wp2-trust-specifications/latest/sections/references/) therein, and the ARF.

The purpose of this RFC is to establish a stable starting point for interoperability across APTITUDE pilots by defining:

- impacted roles and components;
- normative requirements for X.509 chain revocation checking of WRPAC and Sign/Seal certificates and Status List Token validation of WRPRCs, WIAs, KAs, and Digital Credentials (PID/QEAA, EAA, and PuB-EAA);
- interface definitions for the relevant protocols (OCSP, CRL, Status List Token);
- a conformance check catalogue that maps the normative requirements into verifiable checks;
- a test case matrix that maps the conformance checks into executable test cases for the **ITB+ conformance test cases**.

This RFC is intended to support interoperability first. Pilot-specific or sector-specific extensions may be added later, but they should not weaken the baseline requirements defined here.

---

## **2\. Scope**

This RFC profiles status establishment and revocation checking under three normative tracks:

- **OCSP track** -- X.509 chain revocation checking using RFC 6960. Wallet support is mandatory for WRPAC and Sign/Seal certificate chains, and RP support is mandatory for Sign/Seal certificate chains; OCSP is used when the certificate advertises an `id-ad-ocsp` access location and OCSP checking is selected;
- **CRL track** -- X.509 chain revocation checking using RFC 5280. Wallet support is mandatory for WRPAC and Sign/Seal certificate chains, and RP support is mandatory for Sign/Seal certificate chains; a CRL is used when the certificate advertises a `cRLDistributionPoints` location and CRL checking is selected;
- **TSL track** -- Wallet status checking for WRPRCs and Digital Credentials, RP status checking for Digital Credentials, and Credential Issuer status establishment or checking for WIAs and KAs, profiled under the applicable APTITUDE Rulebooks, ETSI profiles and the [IETF Token Status List draft-21](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-oauth-status-list/21/).

In this RFC, **X.509 chain revocation checking** means determining the revocation status of every certificate in the relevant X.509 certification path, including the WRPAC or Sign/Seal certificate and any revocable issuing or intermediate certificates. This checking is used to validate whether the WRPAC or Sign/Seal certificate can be trusted for the intended operation. Wallets support both CRL and OCSP checking for WRPAC and Sign/Seal certificate chains, and RPs support both mechanisms for Sign/Seal certificate chains. Each validator uses an advertised mechanism for every revocable certificate. If no status endpoint is available, the certificate status is `unknown` and the certificate is considered untrusted.

This RFC includes baseline requirements for:

- generation of OCSP Requests;
- generation of OCSP Response and their verification;
- CRL parsing and verification;
- Status List Token parsing and verification for WRPRCs, WIAs, KAs, and Digital Credentials.

This RFC does **not** define:

- sector-specific certificate semantics (already defined in [WRPAC profile](https://aptitude-consortium.github.io/wp2-trust-specifications/latest/sections/trust-artifacts/#wallet-relying-party-access-certificate) and [WRPRC profile](https://aptitude-consortium.github.io/wp2-trust-specifications/latest/sections/trust-artifacts/#wallet-relying-party-registration-certificate));
- the lifecycle of the entities and related Trust Artifacts (already defined in [Trust Management](https://aptitude-consortium.github.io/wp2-trust-specifications/latest/sections/trust-management-lifecycle/#trust-management-process));
- revocation artifacts and semantics (already defined in [Revocation Mechanisms](https://aptitude-consortium.github.io/wp2-trust-specifications/latest/sections/trust-management-lifecycle/#revocation-mechanisms));
- pilot-specific UI requirements;
- detailed test assertions; (these are derived into the *ITB+* test suite from the requirements matrix in this document).

## **4\. Roles and Components**

### **4.1 Wallet-Relying Party**

The **<roles:Wallet-Relying Party (WRP)|Wallet-Relying Party>** is defined in the APTITUDE glossary and aligned with the ARF notions of Relying Party, Attestation Provider and PID Provider. In this RFC, a <roles:Relying Party (RP)|Relying Party> (RP) checks the status of Sign/Seal certificate chains and Digital Credentials it receives.

### **4.2 Wallet Unit**

The **Wallet Unit** is the <components:Wallet Unit> as defined in the ARF and APTITUDE glossary, including one or more <components:Wallet Instance|Wallet Instances> on the Holder’s device or environment. In this RFC, the term **Wallet** refers to the Wallet Unit (and its Wallet Instances) when the distinction is not material, acting on behalf of the <roles:User|Holder> to perform X.509 chain revocation checking for WRPAC and Sign/Seal certificates and Status List Token validation for WRPRCs and Digital Credentials.

### **4.3 Provider of WRPAC**

The **<roles:Provider of Wallet-Relying Party Access Certificate (Provider of WRPAC)|Provider of WRPAC>**, as defined in the APTITUDE glossary and aligned with the ARF notion of Provider of WRPAC, is the entity issuing WRPAC and providing the CRL endpoint or OCSP responder advertised by each revocable WRPAC.

### **4.4 Provider of WRPRC**

The **<roles:Provider of Wallet-Relying Party Registration Certificate (Provider of WRPRC)|Provider of WRPRC>**, as defined in the APTITUDE glossary and aligned with the ARF notion of Provider of WRPRC, is the entity issuing WRPRC and the related status Services (Status List Token endpoint).

### **4.5 Wallet Provider**

The **<roles:Wallet Provider (WP)|Wallet Provider> (WP)**, as defined in the APTITUDE glossary and aligned with the ARF notion of Wallet Provider, is the entity providing Wallet Solutions. For the purposes of this RFC, the WP is responsible for issuing and cryptographically signing Wallet Unit Attestations, including WIAs and KAs, and for managing their lifecycle and revocation through Status List Tokens.

### **4.6 Attestation Provider**

The **<roles:Attestation Provider (AP)|Attestation Provider> (AP)**, as defined in the APTITUDE glossary and aligned with the ARF notion of Attestation Provider, is the entity issuing Digital Credentials. For the purposes of this RFC, the applicable AP acts as the Credential Issuer: it establishes or checks WIA and KA status and is responsible for issuing and cryptographically signing PID, QEAA, EAA, and PuB-EAA attestations and managing their lifecycle and revocation through Status List Tokens.

### **4.7 Provider of Sign/Seal Certificates**

The **Provider of Sign/Seal Certificates** is the Certificate Authority or Qualified Trust Service Provider responsible for issuing and managing the Sign/Seal Certificates used by Wallet Providers, PID Providers, and Attestation Providers. Sign/Seal Certificates and their certificate paths are profiled in the applicable EUDI Wallet certificate-profile specifications, including ETSI TS 119 412-6.

In this RFC, the Provider of Sign/Seal Certificates is responsible for ensuring that the revocation status of every revocable certificate in a Sign/Seal certificate path can be checked using an advertised CRL distribution point or OCSP responder. Both mechanisms are supported by Wallet Units and RPs, but a certificate need only provide one of them.

## **6\. Normative Requirements**

This section defines the baseline normative requirements for APTITUDE implementations of the status-establishment and revocation-checking profile.

Unless otherwise stated, the requirements in this section apply whenever:

- a Wallet checks the status of a WRPAC or Sign/Seal certificate, a WRPRC, or a Digital Credential;
- an RP checks the status of a Sign/Seal certificate or Digital Credential; or
- a Credential Issuer establishes or checks the status of a WIA or KA.

The primary use cases are a Wallet Instance or RP validating trust artifacts or attestations, and a Credential Issuer establishing or checking WIA or KA status during issuance.

### **6.1 Wallet Solution Requirements**

A Wallet Solution conforming to this RFC SHALL:

- Support RFC 5280 for CRL parsing and validation of WRPAC and Sign/Seal certificate chains;
- Support RFC 6960 for OCSP request generation and response parsing and validation of WRPAC and Sign/Seal certificate chains;
- check the revocation status of every revocable certificate in a WRPAC or Sign/Seal certificate path using a CRL advertised in `cRLDistributionPoints` or an OCSP responder advertised by `id-ad-ocsp` in `authorityInfoAccess`; when both are advertised, either mechanism MAY be used; if neither mechanism has an available endpoint, the Wallet SHALL consider the certificate status `unknown` and the certificate untrusted;
- Support the [IETF Token Status List draft-21](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-oauth-status-list/21/) for Status List Token parsing and validation for WRPRCs and Digital Credentials;
- support JWT and CWT representations of Status List Tokens as required by the applicable TSL profile;
- when checking a WRPRC or Digital Credential, retrieve and validate its Status List Token and apply the status mapping and acceptance decision defined by the applicable profile.

### **6.2 Provider of WRPAC Requirements**

A WRPAC Provider conforming to this RFC SHALL:

- advertise a `cRLDistributionPoints` location or an `id-ad-ocsp` access location in `authorityInfoAccess` for each revocable WRPAC;
- expose a CRL endpoint as described in section [CRL Endpoint](#71-crl-endpoint) and generate CRLs with the semantics defined in [CRL](https://aptitude-consortium.github.io/wp2-trust-specifications/latest/sections/trust-management-lifecycle/#certificate-revocation-lists) when a CRL distribution point is advertised;
- expose an OCSP endpoint as described in section [OCSP Endpoint](#72-ocsp-endpoint) with responses conforming to [OCSP response](https://aptitude-consortium.github.io/wp2-trust-specifications/latest/sections/trust-artifacts/#ocsp-response) when an OCSP responder is advertised.

### **6.3 Provider of WRPRC Requirements**

A WRPRC Provider conforming to this RFC SHALL:

- expose a Status List Token endpoint as described in section [SLT Endpoint](#73-tsl-endpoint);
- act as both the Status Issuer and the Status Provider for its WRPRCs;
- generate and maintain the Status List, listing the status of all issued WRPRCs, and embed it into a Status List Token as defined in the [WRPRC Token Status List Profile](https://aptitude-consortium.github.io/wp2-trust-specifications/latest/sections/trust-management-lifecycle/#token-status-list-profiles) and the [IETF Token Status List draft-21](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-oauth-status-list/21/);
- ensure that revocation of a WRPRC is reflected in the Status List without undue delay.

### **6.4 Wallet Provider Requirements**

A Wallet Provider conforming to this RFC SHALL:

- issue and cryptographically sign the WIAs and KAs that it provides;
- allocate each issued WIA and KA a distinct, non-negative `idx` value and include the corresponding Status List reference in the location defined by the [Wallet Unit Attestation Profile](#1011-token-status-list-wallet-unit-attestation-profile);
- implement the Wallet Unit Attestation TSL profile in [Section 10.1.1](#1011-token-status-list-wallet-unit-attestation-profile), including its status encoding, SLT generation, signing, compression, and publication requirements;
- act as both the Status Issuer and the Status Provider for its WIAs and KAs;
- expose a Status List Token endpoint as described in section [SLT Endpoint](#73-tsl-endpoint);
- generate and maintain Status Lists containing the status of its issued WIAs and KAs, and embed them into Status List Tokens as defined in the [IETF Token Status List draft-21](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-oauth-status-list/21/);
- manage the lifecycle and revocation of its WIAs and KAs and reflect each status change in the applicable Status List without undue delay.

### **6.5 Attestation Provider Requirements**

An Attestation Provider conforming to this RFC SHALL:

- when acting as a Credential Issuer, support the [IETF Token Status List draft-21](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-oauth-status-list/21/) and the [Wallet Unit Attestation Profile](#1011-token-status-list-wallet-unit-attestation-profile) for establishing or checking WIA and KA status, including JWT and CWT Status List Token representations as required by that profile;
- when establishing or checking WIA or KA status, retrieve and validate the referenced Status List Token and apply the status mapping and acceptance decision in the [Wallet Unit Attestation Profile](#1011-token-status-list-wallet-unit-attestation-profile);
- issue and cryptographically sign the issued PID, QEAA, EAA, and PuB-EAA depending on the type of Digital Credentials it provides;
- allocate each issued Digital Credential a distinct, non-negative `idx` value and include the corresponding Status List reference in the location defined by the [Digital Credentials Profile](#1012-token-status-list-digital-credentials-profile);
- implement the Digital Credentials TSL profile in [Section 10.1.2](#1012-token-status-list-digital-credentials-profile), including its SLT generation, signing, compression, and publication requirements; the Credential Issuer SHOULD use the 2-bit status encoding profiled there and, if it uses a different encoding or status mapping, SHALL specify that encoding and mapping and ensure that other entities processing its Digital Credentials can process them;
- act as both the Status Issuer and the Status Provider for its Digital Credentials;
- expose a Status List Token endpoint as described in section [SLT Endpoint](#73-tsl-endpoint);
- generate and maintain Status Lists containing the status of its issued Digital Credentials, and embed them into Status List Tokens as defined in the [IETF Token Status List draft-21](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-oauth-status-list/21/);
- manage the lifecycle and revocation of its Digital Credentials and reflect each status change in the applicable Status List without undue delay.

### **6.6 Provider of Sign/Seal Certificate Requirements**

A Provider of Sign/Seal Certificates conforming to this RFC SHALL:

- issue and manage Sign/Seal Certificates in accordance with the applicable EUDI Wallet certificate profiles;
- ensure that a CRL distribution point or an OCSP responder is available for every revocable certificate in each Sign/Seal certificate path;
- include a `cRLDistributionPoints` location for a publicly available CRL in each Sign/Seal certificate for which the Provider is responsible if no OCSP responder is advertised for that certificate;
- include an `id-ad-ocsp` access location in the Authority Information Access extension of each Sign/Seal certificate for which the Provider is responsible if OCSP is supported for that certificate;
- provide CRLs conforming to [CRL](https://aptitude-consortium.github.io/wp2-trust-specifications/latest/sections/trust-management-lifecycle/#certificate-revocation-lists) when CRL distribution points are advertised, and generate OCSP responses conforming to [OCSP response](https://aptitude-consortium.github.io/wp2-trust-specifications/latest/sections/trust-artifacts/#ocsp-response), including the required freshness and nonce-processing rules, when OCSP responders are advertised;
- omit `noRevAvail` and `ext-etsi-valassured-ST-certs` from Sign/Seal certificates;
- ensure that revocation of a Sign/Seal certificate or a revocable certificate in its certification path is reflected by each advertised revocation service without undue delay.

**Note:** A Provider of Sign/Seal Certificates MAY provide both CRL and OCSP services for a certificate. Neither service is required for every Sign/Seal certificate when the other is advertised.

### **6.7 Relying Party Requirements**

An RP conforming to this RFC SHALL:

- support RFC 5280 for CRL parsing and validation of Sign/Seal certificate chains;
- support RFC 6960 for OCSP request generation and response parsing and validation of Sign/Seal certificate chains;
- check the revocation status of every revocable certificate in those paths using a CRL advertised in `cRLDistributionPoints` or an OCSP responder advertised by `id-ad-ocsp` in `authorityInfoAccess`; when both are advertised, either mechanism MAY be used; if neither mechanism has an available endpoint, the RP SHALL consider the certificate status `unknown` and the certificate untrusted;
- support the [IETF Token Status List draft-21](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-oauth-status-list/21/) and the specific [SLT Digital Credentials Profile](#1012-token-status-list-digital-credentials-profile) for checking Digital Credential status, including JWT and CWT Status List Token representations as required by that profile;
- when checking a Digital Credential, retrieve and validate its Status List Token and apply the status mapping and acceptance decision defined by its profile.

## **7\. Interface Definitions**

This section defines the logical interfaces for conformance testing. The following interfaces are added on top of these added in RFC001, RFC002.

### **7.1 CRL Endpoint**

- **Direction**: 
\
  - Wallet for WRPAC or Sign/Seal certificates; or 
  - RP for Sign/Seal certificates,

 to the CRL endpoint identified for the certificate being checked.

- **Method**: `GET`

This endpoint is used for CRL-based X.509 chain revocation checking of WRPAC and Sign/Seal certificate chains by Wallets and Sign/Seal certificate chains by RPs. Each validator SHALL support this interface for its applicable chain type and SHALL use it when CRL checking is selected for a certificate with a `cRLDistributionPoints` location.

#### **Request**

The Wallet or RP SHALL construct an HTTP `GET` request to retrieve the CRL associated with the certificate being checked in its applicable certificate chain.

- **URI Construction**:

  - The request URI is extracted directly from the `cRLDistributionPoints` extension of the certificate.
  - No additional query parameters are required unless explicitly specified by the URI in the CDP extension.

- **Headers**:

  - `Host`: SHALL contain the hostname from the CRL URI.
  - `Accept`: SHALL contain the value `application/pkix-crl`.

#### **Response**

The Provider's CRL endpoint SHALL respond with the appropriate HTTP status codes and the correctly formatted CRL file if the request is successful.

- **Status Codes**:

  - `200 OK`: The request was successful, and the CRL is included in the response body.
  - `4xx` / `5xx`: Standard HTTP error codes (e.g., `404 Not Found`, `500 Internal Server Error`) SHALL be used only if the server cannot process the HTTP request at the transport level.

- **Headers**:

  - `Content-Type`: SHALL be set to `application/pkix-crl` for successful responses containing the CRL.
  - `Content-Length`: SHOULD be included to indicate the size of the CRL file in bytes.

- **Body**:

  - The response body SHALL contain the CRL in ASN.1 DER format as specified in [RFC 5280](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc5280).

### **7.2 OCSP Endpoint**

- **Direction**: 

  - Wallet for WRPAC or Sign/Seal certificates; or
  - RP for Sign/Seal certificates, 
 
 to the OCSP responder identified for the certificate being checked.

- **Method**: `POST`

Wallets SHALL support this interface for WRPAC and Sign/Seal certificate chains, and RPs SHALL support it for Sign/Seal certificate chains. They SHALL use it when OCSP checking is selected for a certificate with an `id-ad-ocsp` access location.

[OPTIONAL] If the DER encoding of the request is less than 255 bytes, the Wallet or RP MAY use the `GET` method as defined in RFC 6960, by encoding the OCSP request in the URL. In this case, the interface is defined as follows:

- **Direction**: Wallet or RP to the OCSP responder
- **Method**: `GET`

#### **Request**

As detailed in [OCSP request](https://aptitude-consortium.github.io/wp2-trust-specifications/latest/sections/trust-artifacts/#ocsp-request), the OCSP request is a DER encoded `OCSPRequest` structure, with the following parameters:

- `version`;
- `requestList` with a list of `Request` structures, each containing:

  - `reqCert` with the `CertID` of the certificate to be checked;

- `requestExtensions` with the `id-pkix-ocsp-nonce` extension containing a fresh nonce value.

The Wallet or RP SHALL construct an HTTP `POST` request to the URI extracted from the Authority Information Access extension of the certificate being checked, specifically the `accessLocation` associated with the `id-ad-ocsp` access method.

- **POST Headers**: The request SHALL include a `Content-Type` header set to `application/ocsp-request` and an `Accept` header set to `application/ocsp-response`.
- **POST Body**: The body of the `POST` request SHALL contain the binary DER-encoded `OCSPRequest` structure.
- **GET Construction (Optional)**: If utilizing the optional `GET` method, the request URI SHALL be constructed by appending a URL-encoded Base64 representation of the DER-encoded `OCSPRequest` to the base OCSP URI (e.g., {OCSP_URI}/{base64_encoded_request}). The GET request SHALL include an `Accept` header set to `application/ocsp-response` and has no message body.

#### **Response**

As detailed in [OCSP response](https://aptitude-consortium.github.io/wp2-trust-specifications/latest/sections/trust-artifacts/#ocsp-response), the OCSP response is a DER encoded `OCSPResponse` structure, with the following parameters:

- `responseStatus` with the status of the response;
- `responseBytes` with the `responseType` and the `response` containing the DER encoded `BasicOCSPResponse` structure, which includes:

  - `tbsResponseData` with the list of `SingleResponse` structures, each containing:

    - `certID` matching the request;
    - `certStatus` with the status of the certificate (`good`, `revoked`, or `unknown`);
    - `thisUpdate` and optionally `nextUpdate` for temporal validity checks.

  - `signatureAlgorithm` and `signatureValue` for cryptographic validation;
  - `responseExtensions` with the `id-pkix-ocsp-nonce` extension containing the same nonce value as in the request.

If the request is successful, the OCSP responder SHALL respond with the appropriate HTTP status codes and the correctly formatted OCSP response:

- **Status Codes**:

  - `200 OK`: The request was successfully received and processed. Note that a 200 OK HTTP status SHALL be returned even if the internal OCSP `responseStatus` indicates an error (e.g., `malformedRequest`, `internalError`, `tryLater`), provided the HTTP transport layer itself succeeded.
  - `4xx` / `5xx`: Standard HTTP error codes (e.g., `404 Not Found`, `500 Internal Server Error`) SHALL be used only if the server cannot process the HTTP request at the transport level.

- **Headers**:

  - `Content-Type`: SHALL be set to `application/ocsp-response`.
  - `Content-Length`: SHALL accurately reflect the size of the DER-encoded OCSP response in bytes.
  - `Cache-Control` / `Expires`: MAY be included to govern HTTP caching behaviors, in case it SHALL align with the `nextUpdate` field provided in the internal payload.

- **Body Format**:

  - The HTTP response body SHALL contain the binary DER-encoded `OCSPResponse` structure.

### **7.3 TSL Endpoint**

- **Direction**: 

  - Wallet for WRPRCs or Digital Credentials;
  - RP for Digital Credentials; or
  - Credential Issuer for WIAs or KAs, 
  
 to the applicable Status Provider

- **Method**: `GET`

This endpoint is used for Wallet validation of WRPRC and Digital Credential status, RP validation of Digital Credential status, and Credential Issuer establishment or checking of WIA and KA status.

#### **Request**

The Wallet, RP, or Credential Issuer SHALL construct an HTTP `GET` request to retrieve the Status List Token for its applicable artifact.

- **URI Construction**:

  - The request URI SHALL be extracted from the applicable Status List reference of the token or credential being verified. The reference location and syntax SHALL be determined according to the applicable profile in [Section 10.1](#101-conformance-check-catalogue).

- **Headers**:

  - `Host`: SHALL contain the hostname extracted from the Status List URI.
  - `Accept`: SHOULD include `application/statuslist+jwt` and/or `application/statuslist+cwt` to indicate the requester's supported Status List Token representations.

#### **Response**

The Status Provider's endpoint SHALL respond with the appropriate HTTP status codes and the correctly formatted Status List Token.

- **Status Codes**:

  - `200 OK`: The request was successful, and the Status List Token is included in the response body.
  - `4xx` / `5xx`: Standard HTTP error codes (e.g., `404 Not Found`, `500 Internal Server Error`) SHALL be used only if the server cannot process the HTTP request at the transport level.

- **Headers**:

  - `Content-Type`: SHALL be set to `application/statuslist+jwt` for a JWT SLT or `application/statuslist+cwt` for a CWT SLT.

- **Body**:

  - The response body SHALL consist of a JWT or CWT Status List Token.
  - The Status List Token SHALL contain the claims defined in [Status List Token](https://aptitude-consortium.github.io/wp2-trust-specifications/latest/sections/trust-artifacts/#status-list-token).

## **8\. Privacy and Security Considerations**

### **8.1 Privacy Considerations**

While WRPACs, WRPRCs, WIAs, KAs, Digital Credentials, and Sign/Seal Certificates identify organizational entities or Wallet components and need not contain Personally Identifiable Information (PII), status queries for these artifacts can reveal information about the transaction to the status provider.

- When a Wallet checks a WRPAC or Sign/Seal certificate or an RP checks a Sign/Seal certificate through a live OCSP request, the serial number of that certificate is transmitted over the network. A network observer can correlate the OCSP request with the relevant organization, revealing the user's interactions.
- To obscure the specific endpoints being queried and the parameters of the request, Wallets and RPs SHOULD mandate the use of HTTPS (TLS) for their respective CRL and OCSP network queries; Wallets, RPs, and Credential Issuers SHOULD do so for their applicable TSL queries, despite the payloads already being cryptographically signed.
- For WRPAC chains that advertise both mechanisms, implementers concerned with high-privacy environments SHOULD favor the use of cached CRLs over real-time OCSP queries. By downloading a batched list of statuses, the Wallet obfuscates the specific certificate it is actively verifying, blending the user's specific query into a larger cryptographic dataset. For Sign/Seal certificate chains, a cached CRL MAY be used when the certificate advertises a CRL distribution point.
- To prevent Wallet Providers from tracking or profiling Users based on their use of Wallet Unit Attestations, Wallet Providers MUST integrate the status information for many WIAs or KAs into the same Status List and MUST publish the SLT at the same `uri` for all those attestations. Wallet Providers MUST configure Status Lists with at least 100,000 status entries. If more attestations are issued, the Wallet Provider MAY create additional SLTs or increase the number of entries in the array, depending on practical considerations such as the total size of each SLT and the management of multiple endpoints.
- To prevent Attestation Providers from tracking or profiling Users based on their use of Digital Credentials, Attestation Providers MUST integrate the status information for many PID, QEAA, EAA, or PuB-EAA Digital Credentials into the same Status List and MUST publish the SLT at the same `uri` for all those attestations. Attestation Providers MUST configure Status Lists with at least 100,000 status entries. If more Digital Credentials are issued, the Attestation Provider MAY create additional SLTs or increase the number of entries in the array, depending on practical considerations such as the total size of each SLT and the management of multiple endpoints.

### **8.2 Security Considerations**

Implementations of this specification SHALL account for multiple attack vectors against the PKI and revocation infrastructure. The following considerations establish the baseline security posture for Wallets and Providers of certificates.

#### **8.1.1 Cryptographic Integrity and Trust Anchors**

Implementations SHALL NOT rely on deprecated cryptographic algorithms (e.g., SHA-1, RSA-1024) for the signature generation or validation of CRLs, OCSP responses, or Status List Tokens (SLTs).

For X.509 chain revocation checking, Wallets checking WRPAC or Sign/Seal certificates and RPs checking Sign/Seal certificates SHALL thoroughly validate the certification path of the OCSP responder or CRL issuer up to a trusted root, verifying that the signer is explicitly authorized to issue revocation data for the certificate being checked (e.g., checking the `cRLSign` bit or `id-kp-OCSPSigning` Extended Key Usage).

#### **8.1.2 Freshness and Replay Attacks**

To prevent attackers from capturing a valid `good` OCSP response and replaying it after a certificate has been revoked, Wallets and RPs SHALL include the `id-pkix-ocsp-nonce` in requests and strictly enforce its matching presence in the response.

Wallets and RPs SHALL strictly validate the `thisUpdate` and `nextUpdate` fields of CRLs and OCSP responses for their applicable certificate chains. Wallets, RPs, and Credential Issuers SHALL also validate the `iat` and `exp` fields of Status List Tokens for their applicable artifacts. Systems SHOULD account for minor acceptable clock skew between the Provider and the validator, but SHALL reject responses where temporal validity has unambiguously expired, as these may indicate a downgrade or replay attack.

#### **8.1.3 Denial of Service (DoS)**

Providers of WRPAC and WRPRC, Wallet Providers, and Attestation Providers SHALL ensure high availability of each CRL, OCSP, or TSL endpoint they expose. Providers of Sign/Seal Certificates SHALL ensure high availability of each CRL endpoint or OCSP responder advertised for their Sign/Seal certificates. If no certificate-status endpoint is available, including when an advertised endpoint is unreachable, the Wallet or RP SHALL assign the certificate status `unknown` and consider the certificate untrusted.

#### **8.1.4 Transport Security**

While the integrity of CRLs, OCSP responses, and TSLs is guaranteed by their digital signatures rather than the transport layer, the use of plain HTTP exposes the connection to manipulation. Attackers could intercept requests and return TCP RST packets or spoof 404 Not Found or 500 Internal Server Error responses, preventing the applicable Wallet, RP, or Credential Issuer from evaluating the cryptographic payload.

## **9\. Conformance**

An implementation **conforms to this specification as a Wallet** if it:

1. implements the requirements defined in [Section 6.1](#61-wallet-solution-requirements);
2. supports the CRL and OCSP interfaces defined in [Section 7](#7-interface-definitions) for WRPAC and Sign/Seal certificate chains, and the TSL interface for WRPRCs and Digital Credentials;
3. successfully passes the applicable conformance checks defined in the [Conformance Check Catalogue](#101-conformance-check-catalogue), including both CRL and OCSP checks for WRPAC and Sign/Seal certificate chains and TSL checks for WRPRCs and Digital Credentials.

An implementation **conforms to this specification as an RP** if it:

1. implements the requirements defined in [Section 6.7](#67-relying-party-requirements);
2. supports the CRL and OCSP interfaces defined in [Section 7](#7-interface-definitions) for Sign/Seal certificate chains and the TSL interface for Digital Credentials;
3. successfully passes the applicable CRL and OCSP conformance checks defined in the [Conformance Check Catalogue](#101-conformance-check-catalogue) for Sign/Seal certificate chains and the TSL checks for Digital Credentials.

An implementation **conforms to this specification as a Provider of WRPAC** if it:

1. implements the requirements defined in [Section 6.2](#62-provider-of-wrpac-requirements);
2. supports the CRL and/or OCSP interfaces defined in [Section 7](#7-interface-definitions) according to the revocation locations advertised in its WRPACs;

An implementation **conforms to this specification as a Provider of WRPRC** if it:

1. implements the requirements defined in [Section 6.3](#63-provider-of-wrprc-requirements);
2. supports the interfaces defined in [Section 7](#7-interface-definitions) for the TSL track;

An implementation **conforms to this specification as a Wallet Provider** if it:

1. implements the requirements defined in [Section 6.4](#64-wallet-provider-requirements);
2. supports the TSL interface defined in [Section 7](#7-interface-definitions) for WIAs and KAs;

An implementation **conforms to this specification as an Attestation Provider** if it:

1. implements the requirements defined in [Section 6.5](#65-attestation-provider-requirements);
2. supports the TSL interface defined in [Section 7](#7-interface-definitions) as a Status Provider for PID, QEAA, EAA, and PuB-EAA Digital Credentials and, when acting as a Credential Issuer, as a status checker for WIAs and KAs;
3. successfully passes the applicable TSL conformance checks in the [Conformance Check Catalogue](#101-conformance-check-catalogue) for establishing or checking WIA and KA status;

An implementation **conforms to this specification as a Provider of Sign/Seal Certificates** if it:

1. implements the requirements defined in [Section 6.6](#66-provider-of-signseal-certificate-requirements);
2. supports the CRL and/or OCSP interfaces defined in [Section 7](#7-interface-definitions) according to the revocation locations advertised for every revocable certificate in its Sign/Seal certificate paths.

## **10. Conformance Catalogue and Deployed Test Matrix**

### **10.1 Conformance Check Catalogue**

This catalogue defines the baseline conformance checks that support RFC interoperability claims under [Section 9](#9-conformance). These checks map directly to the normative requirements defined in Sections [6](#6-normative-requirements) and [7](#7-interface-definitions).

The CRL and OCSP checks apply to Wallets for WRPAC and Sign/Seal certificate chains and to RPs for Sign/Seal certificate chains. Conformance requires support for both mechanisms, with each revocable certificate checked using an advertised mechanism. A certificate with no available status endpoint is `unknown` and untrusted. Provider checks apply to the mechanisms advertised in the certificates they issue. The TSL checks apply to Wallets for WRPRCs and Digital Credentials, RPs for Digital Credentials, and Credential Issuers for WIAs and KAs.

The [WRPRC Token Status List Profile](https://aptitude-consortium.github.io/wp2-trust-specifications/latest/sections/trust-management-lifecycle/#token-status-list-profiles) defines the WRPRC-specific status encoding and processing. Sections 10.1.1 and 10.1.2 below profile WIAs, KAs, and Digital Credentials.

#### **10.1.1 Token Status List (Wallet Unit Attestation Profile)**

This section profiles the Token Status List (TSL) mechanism of the [IETF Token Status List draft-21](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-oauth-status-list/21/) for Wallet Unit Attestations. A TSL conveys the current status of many Wallet Unit Attestations in a compact, signed Status List Token (SLT).

The SLT Provider MUST be the Wallet Provider.

<!-- markdownlint-disable-next-line MD036 -->
**Status List**

A Status List contains a compressed byte array whose entries represent the statuses of many Wallet Instance Attestations (WIAs) or Key Attestations (KAs). A WIA MUST include its Status List reference in `client_status.status.status_list`, and a KA MUST include its Status List reference in `key_storage_status.status.status_list`. Each Status List reference MUST contain `idx` and `uri`. Both WIAs and KAs are JWTs; therefore, `idx` is a JSON integer and `uri` is a JSON string in each reference. In both cases, `uri` MUST be a URI conforming to [RFC 3986](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3986).

According to this specification, a WIA or KA can have one of the following statuses:

- `VALID`. The WIA or KA is valid. This status is represented by `0x00` in the SLT.
- `INVALID`. The WIA or KA is revoked. This status is represented by `0x01` in the SLT.

As a result, the Wallet Provider MUST set the `bits` parameter in the SLT's `status_list` object to `1`.

The Wallet Provider MUST pack entries starting with the least significant bit of each byte, compress the byte array using DEFLATE with the ZLIB data format, and publish the resulting Status List in the SLT.

<!-- markdownlint-disable-next-line MD036 -->
**Status List Token**

The Wallet Provider MUST act as both the Status Issuer and the Status Provider. It MUST make each SLT available via HTTP `GET` at the URI specified by either the WIA's `client_status.status.status_list.uri` member or the KA's `key_storage_status.status.status_list.uri` member, using `application/statuslist+jwt` for a JWT SLT or `application/statuslist+cwt` for a CWT SLT.

The SLT format MAY be either a JWT or a CWT and MUST be protected by a cryptographic signature. Regardless of the chosen format, the SLT MUST conform to Section 5.1 for JWTs or Section 5.2 for CWTs of the [IETF Token Status List draft-21](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-oauth-status-list/21/).

Regardless of the format, the Wallet Provider MUST sign the SLT using one of the following:

- a valid X.509 certificate whose trust chain terminates at the Trust Anchor published in the Wallet Provider's LoTE when it is registered in the EUDIW Trust Framework; or
- a valid key attested by the Wallet Provider's Entity Configuration when it is registered in the National Trust Framework.

#### **10.1.2 Token Status List (Digital Credentials Profile)**

This section profiles the Token Status List (TSL) mechanism of the [IETF Token Status List draft-21](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-oauth-status-list/21/) for Digital Credentials. A TSL conveys the current status of many Digital Credentials in a compact, signed Status List Token (SLT).

The SLT Provider MUST be the Credential Issuer, i.e. the applicable Attestation Provider issuing the Digital Credential.

<!-- markdownlint-disable-next-line MD036 -->
**Status List**

A Status List contains a compressed byte array whose entries represent the statuses of many Digital Credentials. The Credential Issuer MUST allocate each issued Digital Credential a distinct, non-negative `idx` value and include it, together with the SLT `uri`, in the Digital Credential's Status List reference. For an SD-JWT Digital Credential, the reference is in `status.status_list`, `idx` is a JSON integer, and `uri` is a JSON string. For an mdoc MSO, the reference is in `issuerAuth.status_list`, `idx` is a CBOR unsigned integer, and `uri` is a CBOR text string. In both cases, `uri` MUST be a URI conforming to [RFC 3986](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3986).

Under the recommended 2-bit profile, a Digital Credential can have one of the following statuses:

- `VALID`. The Digital Credential is valid. This status is represented by `0x00` in the SLT.
- `INVALID`. The Digital Credential is revoked. This status is represented by `0x01` in the SLT.
- `SUSPENDED`. The Digital Credential is temporarily invalid and may be restored to `VALID`. This status is represented by `0x02` in the SLT.

The Credential Issuer SHOULD set the `bits` parameter in the SLT's `status_list` object to `2` and use the status mapping above.

If a Credential Issuer needs a different `bits` value or status mapping, it SHALL specify the value, each status code and its meaning, and the acceptance behavior. It SHALL ensure that other entities processing its Digital Credentials can process that profile. The Credential Issuer SHOULD carefully consider the information disclosed to RPs when defining additional statuses for the Digital Credential lifecycle.

The Credential Issuer MUST pack entries starting with the least significant bit of each byte, compress the byte array using DEFLATE with the ZLIB data format, and publish the resulting Status List in the SLT.

<!-- markdownlint-disable-next-line MD036 -->
**Status List Token**

The Credential Issuer MUST act as both the Status Issuer and the Status Provider. It MUST make each SLT available via HTTP `GET` at the URI specified by the Digital Credential's Status List reference, using `application/statuslist+jwt` for a JWT SLT or `application/statuslist+cwt` for a CWT SLT.

The SLT format MAY be either a JWT or a CWT and MUST be protected by a cryptographic signature. Regardless of the chosen format, the SLT MUST conform to Section 5.1 for JWTs or Section 5.2 for CWTs of the [IETF Token Status List draft-21](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-oauth-status-list/21/).

Regardless of the format, the Credential Issuer MUST sign the SLT using one of the following:

- a valid X.509 certificate whose trust chain terminates at the Trust Anchor published in the Attestation Provider's LoTE when it is registered in the EUDIW Trust Framework; or
- a valid key attested by the Attestation Provider's Entity Configuration when it is registered in the National Trust Framework.

| **OCSP-VAL-02** | Cryptographic Integrity | Digital Signature | Verification of the signature value across the hash of the DER encoded `ResponseData`. | Signature SHALL be valid; reject response if cryptographic check fails. |
| **OCSP-VAL-03** | Responder Authorization | CA Signature | Check if the response is signed by the CA that issued the certificate in question. | Proceed to status evaluation if the CA signature is verified. |
| **OCSP-VAL-04** | Responder Authorization | Authorized Responder | Verification of `id-kp-OCSPSigning` (OID `{1.3.6.1.5.5.7.3.9}`) in the Extended Key Usage extension of a responder certificate issued directly by the CA. | Proceed if Extended Key Usage is present and the signer matches the intended recipient. |
| **OCSP-VAL-05** | `CertID` Correlation | Request-Response Binding | Match the `CertID` (`hashAlgorithm`, `hashes`, `serialNumber`) in the response to the original request. | Reject if the response does not correspond to the requested certificate. |
| **OCSP-VAL-06** | Temporal Validity | Freshness Check | Comparison of `thisUpdate` against local system time to ensure it is "sufficiently recent". | Reject if `thisUpdate` is in the future or considered stale by local policy. |
| **OCSP-VAL-07** | Temporal Validity | `nextUpdate` Check | Verification that current time is less than `nextUpdate` (if provided). | Reject as unreliable if system time is past `nextUpdate`. |
| **OCSP-VAL-08** | Status Resolution | Final Status Mapping | Retrieval of `certStatus`: `good`, `revoked` (with reason/time), or `unknown`. | Target certificate is accepted only if status is `good`. |
| **OCSP-EXT-01** | Replay Protection | Nonce Validation | Verification that the `nonce` in the response matches the `nonce` sent in the `requestExtensions`. | Reject response if nonces do not match, preventing replay attacks. |
| **TSL-REQ-01** | Standard TSL Retrieval | HTTP GET via an artifact-specific Status List reference | The Wallet for WRPRCs or Digital Credentials, RP for Digital Credentials, or Credential Issuer for WIAs or KAs extracts the URI and index correctly and sends a GET request with the required `Host` header. | Status Provider returns 2xx and the raw Status List Token. |
| **TSL-RES-01** | Valid TSL Response | Provider Successful Serve | Provider returns `200 OK`, a matching JWT or CWT content type, and a Status List Token payload. | Successful resolution of the SLT from the endpoint. |
| **TSL-VAL-01** | Referenced Artifact Pre-check | Artifact Validation | The Wallet validates a WRPRC or Digital Credential, the RP validates a Digital Credential, or the Credential Issuer validates a WIA or KA before checking its status. | Proceed to SLT fetching only if the referenced artifact is valid; otherwise reject. |
| **TSL-VAL-02** | Status List Reference Syntax | Profile-specific Reference | The applicable Wallet, RP, or Credential Issuer validates the reference claims and the `idx` and `uri` types and syntax. | Validation succeeds if the reference adheres to its artifact profile. |
| **TSL-VAL-03** | SLT Integrity | Cryptographic Validation | Signature verification of the SLT (JWT or CWT format) using a trusted and authorized key. | Reject SLT if signature is invalid or undetermined. |
| **TSL-VAL-04** | Subject Binding | URI Consistency | `sub` (JWT) or `2` (CWT) claim in SLT identifies the `uri` in the referenced artifact's Status List reference. | Reject SLT if identifiers are not equal. |
| **TSL-VAL-05** | Temporal Validity | Expiration & Freshness | Verification of `iat`, `exp`, and `ttl` claims; comparison against `current_time`. | Reject SLT if current time is past `exp` or the token is otherwise stale. |
| **TSL-VAL-06** | Parsing & Decompression | SLT Content Extraction | Decompression of the `lst` claim using DEFLATE with the ZLIB data format. | Successful recovery of the uncompressed Status List. |
| **TSL-VAL-07** | Index Boundary Check | Bit Array Access | Verification that the `idx` from the referenced artifact is within the bounds of the decompressed Status List. | Reject the referenced artifact if the index is out of bounds. |
| **TSL-VAL-08** | Profile-specific Status Resolution | Status Type Evaluation | Retrieval of the entry at `idx` and application of the status mapping defined by the applicable profile. | Final decision is made according to the applicable artifact profile. |
| **TSL-WUA-01** | Wallet Unit Attestation Status Resolution | `bits: 1` | The Credential Issuer retrieves the entry at `idx` and maps `0x00` to `VALID` and `0x01` to `INVALID`. | The Credential Issuer accepts a WIA or KA only when its status is `VALID`. |
| **TSL-WRPRC-01** | WRPRC Status Resolution | WRPRC uses the referenced SLT. | CHK-TSL-WRPRC-01 and CHK-TSL-01 through CHK-TSL-12. | The Wallet applies the [WRPRC Token Status List Profile](https://aptitude-consortium.github.io/wp2-trust-specifications/latest/sections/trust-management-lifecycle/#token-status-list-profiles) and accepts only its valid status. |
| **TSL-DC-01** | Recommended Digital Credential Status Resolution | `bits: 2` | The Wallet and RP each retrieve the entry at `idx` and map `0x00` to `VALID`, `0x01` to `INVALID`, and `0x02` to `SUSPENDED`. | Both validators apply the recommended mapping and do not accept `INVALID` or `SUSPENDED` as `VALID`. |
| **TSL-DC-02** | Issuer-specific Digital Credential Status Resolution | Credential Issuer uses a different `bits` value or status mapping. | CHK-TSL-DC-02 and CHK-TSL-12. | The Credential Issuer specifies the encoding, status meanings, and acceptance behavior; Wallets and RPs processing its Digital Credentials can interpret the issuer-specific profile. |
