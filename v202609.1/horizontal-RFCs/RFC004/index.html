    APTITUDE RFC-04 Trust Revocation¶

    Status: Draft Type: Cross-pilot baseline RFC Scope: Artifact Status Establishment Target protocols: OCSP, CRL, Status List Token Related profiles: APTITUDE Trust Specifications Companion RFC: RFC-03 Trust Establishment

    Authors¶

    Andrea Moro (Fondazione Bruno Kessler)

    Reviewers¶

    Nikos Triantafyllou, University of Aegean; Giancarlo Degani, Infocert; Aleksandar Simsic, ICTU.

    1. Introduction¶

    This document defines the APTITUDE Trust Artifact Status Profile for interoperable status establishment and revocation checking across APTITUDE pilots.

    It specifies a baseline, cross-pilot profile for the validation of trust artifacts aligned with the APTITUDE Trust Specifications, the references therein, and the ARF.

    The purpose of this RFC is to establish a stable starting point for interoperability across APTITUDE pilots by defining:

    • impacted roles and components;
    • normative requirements for X.509 chain revocation checking of WRPAC and Sign/Seal certificates and Status List Token validation of WRPRCs, WIAs, KAs, and Digital Credentials (PID/QEAA, EAA, and PuB-EAA);
    • interface definitions for the relevant protocols (OCSP, CRL, Status List Token);
    • a conformance check catalogue that maps the normative requirements into verifiable checks;
    • a test case matrix that maps the conformance checks into executable test cases for the ITB+ conformance test cases.

    This RFC is intended to support interoperability first. Pilot-specific or sector-specific extensions may be added later, but they should not weaken the baseline requirements defined here.

    2. Scope¶

    This RFC profiles status establishment and revocation checking under three normative tracks:

    • OCSP track -- X.509 chain revocation checking using RFC 6960. OCSP is mandatory for Sign/Seal certificate chains and optional for WRPAC chains;
    • CRL track -- X.509 chain revocation checking for WRPAC chains, profiled under ETSI TS 119 411-8 and RFC 5280. CRL support is mandatory for WRPACs;
    • TSL track -- status establishment and revocation checking for WRPRCs, WIAs, KAs, and Digital Credentials (PID, QEAA, EAA, and PuB-EAA), profiled under the applicable APTITUDE Rulebooks, ETSI profiles and the IETF Token Status List draft-21.

    In this RFC, X.509 chain revocation checking means determining the revocation status of every certificate in the relevant X.509 certification path, including the WRPAC or Sign/Seal certificate and any revocable issuing or intermediate certificates. This checking is used to validate whether the WRPAC or Sign/Seal certificate can be trusted for the intended operation. It is performed with mandatory CRL and optional OCSP for WRPAC chains, and mandatory OCSP for Sign/Seal certificate chains.

    This RFC includes baseline requirements for:

    • generation of OCSP Requests;
    • generation of OCSP Response and their verification;
    • CRL parsing and verification;
    • Status List Token parsing and verification for WRPRCs, WIAs, KAs, and Digital Credentials.

    This RFC does not define:

    • sector-specific certificate semantics (already defined in WRPAC profile and WRPRC profile);
    • the lifecycle of the entities and related Trust Artifacts (already defined in Trust Management);
    • revocation artifacts and semantics (already defined in Revocation Menchanisms);
    • pilot-specific UI requirements;
    • detailed test assertions; (these are derived into the ITB+ test suite from the requirements matrix in this document).

    3. Normative Language¶

    The key words SHALL, SHALL NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD NOT, MAY, and OPTIONAL in this document are to be interpreted as described in RFC 2119 and RFC 8174.

    4. Roles and Components¶

    4.1 Wallet Relying Party¶

    The Wallet Relying Party, as defined in the APTITUDE glossary and aligned with the ARF notions of Relying Party, Attestation Provider and PID Provider. In this RFC, it is the entity to whom the WRPAC and WRPRC refers.

    4.2 Wallet Unit¶

    The Wallet Unit is the Wallet Unit as defined in the ARF and APTITUDE glossary, including one or more Wallet Instances on the Holder’s device or environment. In this RFC, the term Wallet refers to the Wallet Unit (and its Wallet Instances) when the distinction is not material, acting on behalf of the Holder to perform X.509 chain revocation checking for WRPAC and Sign/Seal certificates, and Status List Token validation for WRPRCs, WIAs, KAs, and Digital Credentials.

    4.3 Provider of WRPAC¶

    The Provider of WRPAC, as defined in the APTITUDE glossary and aligned with the ARF notion of Provider of WRPAC, is the entity issuing WRPAC and the related status services: a mandatory CRL endpoint and, optionally, an OCSP responder.

    4.4 Provider of WRPRC¶

    The Provider of WRPRC, as defined in the APTITUDE glossary and aligned with the ARF notion of Provider of WRPRC, is the entity issuing WRPRC and the related status Services (Status List Token endpoint).

    4.5 Wallet Provider¶

    The Wallet Provider (WP), as defined in the APTITUDE glossary and aligned with the ARF notion of Wallet Provider, is the entity providing Wallet Solutions. For the purposes of this RFC, the WP is responsible for issuing and cryptographically signing Wallet Unit Attestations, including WIAs and KAs, and for managing their lifecycle and revocation through Status List Tokens.

    4.6 Attestation Provider¶

    The Attestation Provider (AP), as defined in the APTITUDE glossary and aligned with the ARF notion of Attestation Provider, is the entity issuing Digital Credentials. For the purposes of this RFC, the applicable AP is responsible for issuing and cryptographically signing PID, QEAA, EAA, and PuB-EAA attestations and for managing their lifecycle and revocation through Status List Tokens.

    4.7 Provider of Sign/Seal Certificates¶

    The Provider of Sign/Seal Certificates is the Certificate Authority or Qualified Trust Service Provider responsible for issuing and managing the Sign/Seal Certificates used by Wallet Providers, PID Providers, and Attestation Providers. Sign/Seal Certificates and their certificate paths are profiled in the applicable EUDI Wallet certificate-profile specifications, including ETSI TS 119 412-6.

    In this RFC, the Provider of Sign/Seal Certificates is responsible for ensuring that the revocation status of every revocable certificate in a Sign/Seal certificate path can be checked using the mandatory OCSP track. The Provider of Sign/Seal Certificates may also provide CRLs as a supplementary mechanism, but CRL availability does not replace the mandatory OCSP requirement.

    5. High-Level Flows¶

    The high-level flows for X.509 chain revocation checking of WRPAC and Sign/Seal certificates, and for Status List Token validation of WRPRCs, WIAs, KAs, and Digital Credentials, can be found in Revocation Checking.

    6. Normative Requirements¶

    This section defines the baseline normative requirements for APTITUDE implementations of the status-establishment and revocation-checking profile.

    Unless otherwise stated, the requirements in this section apply whenever a Wallet establishes or checks the status of a WRPAC, WRPRC, WIA, KA, Digital Credential, or Sign/Seal certificate. The primary use case is an Entity (either a Wallet Instance or Wallet-Relying Party) validating trust artifacts or attestations during issuance or presentation flows.

    6.1 Wallet Solution Requirements¶

    A Wallet Solution conforming to this RFC SHALL:

    • Support RFC 5280 for CRL parsing and validation of WRPAC certificate chains;
    • Support RFC 6960 for OCSP request generation and response parsing and validation of Sign/Seal certificate chains;
    • Support the IETF Token Status List draft-21 for Status List Token parsing and validation for WRPRCs, WIAs, KAs, and Digital Credentials;
    • support JWT and CWT representations of Status List Tokens as required by the applicable TSL profile;

    A Wallet Solution conforming to this RFC MAY:

    • Support RFC 6960 for optional OCSP request generation and response parsing and validation of WRPAC certificate chains, as defined in OCSP request.

    6.2 Provider of WRPAC Requirements¶

    A WRPAC Provider conforming to this RFC SHALL:

    • expose at least a CRL endpoint as described in section CRL Endpoint;
    • generate the CRL listing all revoked WRPAC certificates covered by the WRPAC certificate chain with the semantics defined in CRL.

    A WRPAC Provider conforming to this RFC MAY:

    • expose the OCSP endpoint as described in section OCSP Endpoint with OCSP responses conforming to OCSP response.

    Note: OCSP support is optional for conformance. A WRPAC Provider that does not expose an OCSP endpoint conforms through the mandatory CRL track.

    6.3 Provider of WRPRC Requirements¶

    A WRPRC Provider conforming to this RFC SHALL:

    • expose a Status List Token endpoint as described in section SLT Endpoint;
    • act as both the Status Issuer and the Status Provider for its WRPRCs;
    • generate and maintain the Status List, listing the status of all issued WRPRCs, and embed it into a Status List Token as defined in the IETF Token Status List draft-21;
    • ensure that revocation of a WRPRC is reflected in the Status List without undue delay.

    6.4 Wallet Provider Requirements¶

    A Wallet Provider conforming to this RFC SHALL:

    • issue and cryptographically sign the WIAs and KAs that it provides;
    • allocate each issued WIA and KA a distinct, non-negative idx value and include the corresponding Status List reference in the location defined by the Wallet Unit Attestation Profile;
    • implement the Wallet Unit Attestation TSL profile in Section 10.1.1, including its status encoding, SLT generation, signing, compression, and publication requirements;
    • act as both the Status Issuer and the Status Provider for its WIAs and KAs;
    • expose a Status List Token endpoint as described in section SLT Endpoint;
    • generate and maintain Status Lists containing the status of its issued WIAs and KAs, and embed them into Status List Tokens as defined in the IETF Token Status List draft-21;
    • manage the lifecycle and revocation of its WIAs and KAs and reflect each status change in the applicable Status List without undue delay.

    6.5 Attestation Provider Requirements¶

    An Attestation Provider conforming to this RFC SHALL:

    • issue and cryptographically sign the issued PID, QEAA, EAA, and PuB-EAA depending on the type of Digital Credentials it provides;
    • allocate each issued Digital Credential a distinct, non-negative idx value and include the corresponding Status List reference in the location defined by the Digital Credentials Profile;
    • implement the Digital Credentials TSL profile in Section 10.1.2, including its status encoding, SLT generation, signing, compression, and publication requirements;
    • act as both the Status Issuer and the Status Provider for its Digital Credentials;
    • expose a Status List Token endpoint as described in section SLT Endpoint;
    • generate and maintain Status Lists containing the status of its issued Digital Credentials, and embed them into Status List Tokens as defined in the IETF Token Status List draft-21;
    • manage the lifecycle and revocation of its Digital Credentials and reflect each status change in the applicable Status List without undue delay.

    6.6 Provider of Sign/Seal Certificate Requirements¶

    A Provider of Sign/Seal Certificates conforming to this RFC SHALL:

    • issue and manage Sign/Seal Certificates in accordance with the applicable EUDI Wallet certificate profiles;
    • ensure that an OCSP responder is available for every revocable certificate in each Sign/Seal certificate path;
    • include an id-ad-ocsp access location in the Authority Information Access extension of each certificate for which the Provider is responsible;
    • generate OCSP responses conforming to OCSP response, including the required freshness and nonce-processing rules;
    • not use noRevAvail or ext-etsi-valassured-ST-certs to avoid the mandatory OCSP check for Sign/Seal certificate chains;
    • ensure that revocation of a Sign/Seal certificate or a revocable certificate in its certification path is reflected by the mandatory OCSP service without undue delay.

    Note: A Provider of Sign/Seal Certificates MAY additionally expose CRL endpoints. A CRL SHALL be treated as supplementary for Sign/Seal certificate chains and SHALL NOT replace the mandatory OCSP track.

    7. Interface Definitions¶

    This section defines the logical interfaces for conformance testing. The following interfaces are added on top of these added in RFC001, RFC002.

    7.1 CRL Endpoint¶

    • Direction: Wallet to Provider of WRPAC's CRL endpoint.
    • Method: GET

    This endpoint is used for CRL-based X.509 chain revocation checking of WRPAC certificate chains.

    Request¶

    The Wallet SHALL construct an HTTP GET request to retrieve the CRL associated with the certificate being checked in the WRPAC certificate chain.

    • URI Construction:

    • The request URI is extracted directly from the cRLDistributionPoints extension of the certificate.

    • No additional query parameters are required unless explicitly specified by the URI in the CDP extension.

    • Headers:

    • Host: SHALL contain the hostname from the CRL URI.

    • Accept: SHALL contain the value application/pkix-crl.

    Response¶

    The Provider's CRL endpoint SHALL respond with the appropriate HTTP status codes and the correctly formatted CRL file if the request is successful.

    • Status Codes:

    • 200 OK: The request was successful, and the CRL is included in the response body.

    • 4xx / 5xx: Standard HTTP error codes (e.g., 404 Not Found, 500 Internal Server Error) SHALL be used only if the server cannot process the HTTP request at the transport level.

    • Headers:

    • Content-Type: SHALL be set to application/pkix-crl for successful responses containing the CRL.

    • Content-Length: SHOULD be included to indicate the size of the CRL file in bytes.

    • Body:

    • The response body SHALL contain the CRL in ASN.1 DER format as specified in RFC 5270.

    7.2 OCSP Endpoint¶

    • Direction: Wallet to the OCSP responder identified for the certificate being checked.
    • Method: POST

    This endpoint is mandatory for X.509 chain revocation checking of Sign/Seal certificate chains. It is optional for WRPAC certificate chains and is used when a WRPAC Provider exposes the optional OCSP track. The Wallet SHALL perform OCSP checking for each revocable certificate in a Sign/Seal certificate chain and MAY perform it for WRPAC certificate chains.

    [OPTIONAL] If the DER encoding of the request is less than 255 bytes, the Wallet MAY use the GET method as defined in RFC 6960, by encoding the OCSP request in the URL. In this case, the interface is defined as follows:

    • Direction: Wallet to the OCSP responder
    • Method: GET

    Request¶

    As detailed in OCSP request, the OCSP request is a DER encoded OCSPRequest structure, with the following parameters:

    • version;

    • requestList with a list of Request structures, each containing:

    • reqCert with the CertID of the certificate to be checked;

    • requestExtensions with the id-pkix-ocsp-nonce extension containing a fresh nonce value.

    The Wallet SHALL construct an HTTP POST request to the URI extracted from the Authority Information Access extension of the certificate being checked, specifically the accessLocation associated with the id-ad-ocsp access method.

    • POST Headers: The request SHALL include a Content-Type header set to application/ocsp-request and an Accept header set to application/ocsp-response.
    • POST Body: The body of the POST request SHALL contain the binary DER-encoded OCSPRequest structure.
    • GET Construction (Optional): If utilizing the optional GET method, the request URI SHALL be constructed by appending a URL-encoded Base64 representation of the DER-encoded OCSPRequest to the base OCSP URI (e.g., {OCSP_URI}/{base64_encoded_request}). The GET request SHALL include an Accept header set to application/ocsp-response and has no message body.

    Response¶

    As detailed in OCSP response, the OCSP response is a DER encoded OCSPResponse structure, with the following parameters:

    • responseStatus with the status of the response;

    • responseBytes with the responseType and the response containing the DER encoded BasicOCSPResponse structure, which includes:

    • tbsResponseData with the list of SingleResponse structures, each containing:

      • certID matching the request;
      • certStatus with the status of the certificate (good, revoked, or unknown);
      • thisUpdate and optionally nextUpdate for temporal validity checks.

    • signatureAlgorithm and signatureValue for cryptographic validation;

    • responseExtensions with the id-pkix-ocsp-nonce extension containing the same nonce value as in the request.

    If the request is successful, the OCSP responder SHALL respond with the appropriate HTTP status codes and the correctly formatted OCSP response:

    • Status Codes:

    • 200 OK: The request was successfully received and processed. Note that a 200 OK HTTP status SHALL be returned even if the internal OCSP responseStatus indicates an error (e.g., malformedRequest, internalError, tryLater), provided the HTTP transport layer itself succeeded.

    • 4xx / 5xx: Standard HTTP error codes (e.g., 404 Not Found, 500 Internal Server Error) SHALL be used only if the server cannot process the HTTP request at the transport level.

    • Headers:

    • Content-Type: SHALL be set to application/ocsp-response.

    • Content-Length: SHALL accurately reflect the size of the DER-encoded OCSP response in bytes.

    • Cache-Control / Expires: MAY be included to govern HTTP caching behaviors, in case it SHALL align with the nextUpdate field provided in the internal payload.

    • Body Format:

    • The HTTP response body SHALL contain the binary DER-encoded OCSPResponse structure.

    7.3 TSL Endpoint¶

    • Direction: Wallet to the applicable Status Provider
    • Method: GET

    This endpoint is used for Status List Token validation of WRPRCs, WIAs, KAs, and Digital Credentials.

    Request¶

    The Wallet SHALL construct an HTTP GET request to retrieve the Status List Token.

    • URI Construction:

    • The request URI SHALL be extracted from the applicable Status List reference of the token or credential being verified. The reference location and syntax SHALL be determined according to the applicable profile in Section 10.1.

    • Headers:

    • Host: SHALL contain the hostname extracted from the Status List URI.

    • Accept: SHOULD include application/statuslist+jwt and/or application/statuslist+cwt to indicate the Wallet's supported Status List Token representations.

    Response¶

    The Status Provider's endpoint SHALL respond with the appropriate HTTP status codes and the correctly formatted Status List Token.

    • Status Codes:

    • 200 OK: The request was successful, and the Status List Token is included in the response body.

    • 4xx / 5xx: Standard HTTP error codes (e.g., 404 Not Found, 500 Internal Server Error) SHALL be used only if the server cannot process the HTTP request at the transport level.

    • Headers:

    • Content-Type: SHALL be set to application/statuslist+jwt for a JWT SLT or application/statuslist+cwt for a CWT SLT.

    • Body:

    • The response body SHALL consist of a JWT or CWT Status List Token.

    • The Status List Token SHALL contain the claims defined in Status List Token.

    8. Privacy and Security Considerations¶

    8.1 Privacy Considerations¶

    While WRPACs, WRPRCs, WIAs, KAs, Digital Credentials, and Sign/Seal Certificates identify organizational entities or Wallet components and need not contain Personally Identifiable Information (PII), the act of validating these artifacts has significant privacy implications for the end-user operating the Wallet.

    • When a Wallet executes a live OCSP request for a certificate in a WRPAC or Sign/Seal certificate chain, the serial number of that certificate is transmitted over the network. A network observer can correlate the OCSP request with the relevant organization, revealing the user's interactions.
    • To obscure the specific endpoints being queried and the parameters of the request, Wallets SHOULD mandate the use of HTTPS (TLS) for all CRL, OCSP, and TSL network queries, despite the payloads already being cryptographically signed.
    • For WRPAC chains, implementers concerned with high-privacy environments SHOULD favor the use of cached CRLs over real-time OCSP queries. By downloading a batched list of statuses, the Wallet obfuscates the specific certificate it is actively verifying, blending the user's specific query into a larger cryptographic dataset. This recommendation does not replace the mandatory OCSP check for Sign/Seal certificate chains.
    • To prevent Wallet Providers from tracking or profiling Users based on their use of Wallet Unit Attestations, Wallet Providers MUST integrate the status information for many WIAs or KAs into the same Status List and MUST publish the SLT at the same uri for all those attestations. Wallet Providers MUST configure Status Lists with at least 100,000 status entries. If more attestations are issued, the Wallet Provider MAY create additional SLTs or increase the number of entries in the array, depending on practical considerations such as the total size of each SLT and the management of multiple endpoints.
    • To prevent Attestation Providers from tracking or profiling Users based on their use of Digital Credentials, Attestation Providers MUST integrate the status information for many PID, QEAA, EAA, or PuB-EAA Digital Credentials into the same Status List and MUST publish the SLT at the same uri for all those attestations. Attestation Providers MUST configure Status Lists with at least 100,000 status entries. If more Digital Credentials are issued, the Attestation Provider MAY create additional SLTs or increase the number of entries in the array, depending on practical considerations such as the total size of each SLT and the management of multiple endpoints.

    8.2 Security Considerations¶

    Implementations of this specification SHALL account for multiple attack vectors against the PKI and revocation infrastructure. The following considerations establish the baseline security posture for Wallets and Providers of certificates.

    8.1.1 Cryptographic Integrity and Trust Anchors¶

    Implementations SHALL NOT rely on deprecated cryptographic algorithms (e.g., SHA-1, RSA-1024) for the signature generation or validation of CRLs, OCSP responses, or Status List Tokens (SLTs).

    For X.509 chain revocation checking, Wallets SHALL thoroughly validate the certification path of the OCSP responder or CRL issuer up to a trusted root, verifying that the signer is explicitly authorized to issue revocation data for the certificate being checked (e.g., checking the cRLSign bit or id-kp-OCSPSigning Extended Key Usage).

    8.1.2 Freshness and Replay Attacks¶

    To prevent attackers from capturing a valid good OCSP response and replaying it after a certificate has been revoked, Wallets SHALL include the id-pkix-ocsp-nonce in requests and strictly enforce its matching presence in the response.

    Wallets SHALL strictly validate the thisUpdate, nextUpdate, iat, and exp fields across all tracks. Systems SHOULD account for minor acceptable clock skew between the Provider and the Wallet, but SHALL reject responses where temporal validity has unambiguously expired, as these may indicate a downgrade or replay attack.

    8.1.3 Denial of Service (DoS)¶

    Providers of WRPAC and WRPRC, Wallet Providers, and Attestation Providers SHALL ensure high availability of each CRL, OCSP, or TSL endpoint they expose. Providers of Sign/Seal Certificates SHALL ensure high availability of the OCSP responders required for Sign/Seal certificate chains. WRPAC Providers that expose the optional OCSP track SHALL ensure high availability of their OCSP endpoint. Wallets SHALL define a clear policy on how to behave if the revocation endpoint is unreachable (i.e., whether to trust the entity, or abort the transaction).

    8.1.4 Transport Security¶

    While the integrity of CRLs, OCSP responses, and TSLs is guaranteed by their digital signatures rather than the transport layer, the use of plain HTTP exposes the connection to manipulation. Attackers could intercept requests and return TCP RST packets or spoof 404 Not Found or 500 Internal Server Error responses, forcing the Wallet into a fail-open or fail-closed state without ever evaluating the cryptographic payload.

    9. Conformance¶

    An implementation conforms to this specification as a Wallet if it:

    1. implements the requirements defined in Section 6.1;
    2. supports the interfaces defined in Section 7 for CRL-based WRPAC chain checking, OCSP-based Sign/Seal chain checking, and the TSL track;
    3. if it claims support for the optional OCSP track for WRPAC chains, supports the corresponding OCSP interfaces defined in Section 7;
    4. successfully passes all conformance checks defined in the Conformance Check Catalogue for the mandatory tracks and any optional WRPAC OCSP track it claims.

    An implementation conforms to this specification as a Provider of WRPAC if it:

    1. implements the requirements defined in Section 6.2;
    2. supports the mandatory CRL interface defined in Section 7;
    3. if it exposes the optional OCSP track for WRPAC chains, supports the corresponding OCSP interface defined in Section 7;

    An implementation conforms to this specification as a Provider of WRPRC if it:

    1. implements the requirements defined in Section 6.3;
    2. supports the interfaces defined in Section 7 for the TSL track;

    An implementation conforms to this specification as a Wallet Provider if it:

    1. implements the requirements defined in Section 6.4;
    2. supports the TSL interface defined in Section 7 for WIAs and KAs;

    An implementation conforms to this specification as an Attestation Provider if it:

    1. implements the requirements defined in Section 6.5;
    2. supports the TSL interface defined in Section 7 for PID, QEAA, EAA, and PuB-EAA Digital Credentials;

    An implementation conforms to this specification as a Provider of Sign/Seal Certificates if it:

    1. implements the requirements defined in Section 6.6;
    2. supports the mandatory OCSP interface defined in Section 7 for every revocable certificate in its Sign/Seal certificate paths.

    10. Conformance Catalogue and Deployed Test Matrix¶

    10.1 Conformance Check Catalogue¶

    This catalogue defines the baseline conformance checks that support RFC interoperability claims under Section 9. These checks map directly to the normative requirements defined in Sections 6 and 7.

    The CRL checks apply to WRPAC certificate chains, the TSL checks apply to WRPRC, WIA, KA, and Digital Credential status, and the OCSP checks apply mandatorily to Sign/Seal certificate chains. OCSP checks for WRPAC certificate chains apply only to implementations that claim support for the optional WRPAC OCSP track.

    10.1.1 Token Status List (Wallet Unit Attestation Profile)¶

    This section profiles the Token Status List (TSL) mechanism of the IETF Token Status List draft-21 for Wallet Unit Attestations. A TSL conveys the current status of many Wallet Unit Attestations in a compact, signed Status List Token (SLT).

    The SLT Provider MUST be the Wallet Provider.

    Status List

    A Status List contains a compressed byte array whose entries represent the statuses of many Wallet Instance Attestations (WIAs) or Key Attestations (KAs). A WIA MUST include its Status List reference in client_status.status.status_list, and a KA MUST include its Status List reference in key_storage_status.status.status_list. Each Status List reference MUST contain idx and uri. Both WIAs and KAs are JWTs; therefore, idx is a JSON integer and uri is a JSON string in each reference. In both cases, uri MUST be a URI conforming to RFC 3986.

    According to this specification, a WIA or KA can have one of the following statuses:

    • VALID. The WIA or KA is valid. This status is represented by 0x00 in the SLT.
    • INVALID. The WIA or KA is revoked. This status is represented by 0x01 in the SLT.

    As a result, the Wallet Provider MUST set the bits parameter in the SLT's status_list object to 1.

    The Wallet Provider MUST pack entries starting with the least significant bit of each byte, compress the byte array using DEFLATE with the ZLIB data format, and publish the resulting Status List in the SLT.

    Status List Token

    The Wallet Provider MUST act as both the Status Issuer and the Status Provider. It MUST make each SLT available via HTTP GET at the URI specified by either the WIA's client_status.status.status_list.uri member or the KA's key_storage_status.status.status_list.uri member, using application/statuslist+jwt for a JWT SLT or application/statuslist+cwt for a CWT SLT.

    The SLT format MAY be either a JWT or a CWT and MUST be protected by a cryptographic signature. Regardless of the chosen format, the SLT MUST conform to Section 5.1 for JWTs or Section 5.2 for CWTs of the IETF Token Status List draft-21.

    Regardless of the format, the Wallet Provider MUST sign the SLT using one of the following:

    • a valid X.509 certificate whose trust chain terminates at the Trust Anchor published in the Wallet Provider's LoTE when it is registered in the EUDIW Trust Framework; or
    • a valid key attested by the Wallet Provider's Entity Configuration when it is registered in the National Trust Framework.

    10.1.2 Token Status List (Digital Credentials Profile)¶

    This section profiles the Token Status List (TSL) mechanism of the IETF Token Status List draft-21 for Digital Credentials. A TSL conveys the current status of many Digital Credentials in a compact, signed Status List Token (SLT).

    The SLT Provider MUST be the Credential Issuer, i.e. the applicable Attestation Provider issuing the Digital Credential.

    Status List

    A Status List contains a compressed byte array whose entries represent the statuses of many Digital Credentials. The Credential Issuer MUST allocate each issued Digital Credential a distinct, non-negative idx value and include it, together with the SLT uri, in the Digital Credential's Status List reference. For an SD-JWT Digital Credential, the reference is in status.status_list, idx is a JSON integer, and uri is a JSON string. For an mdoc MSO, the reference is in issuerAuth.status_list, idx is a CBOR unsigned integer, and uri is a CBOR text string. In both cases, uri MUST be a URI conforming to RFC 3986.

    According to this specification, a Digital Credential can have one of the following statuses:

    • VALID. The Digital Credential is valid. This status is represented by 0x00 in the SLT.
    • INVALID. The Digital Credential is revoked. This status is represented by 0x01 in the SLT.
    • SUSPENDED. The Digital Credential is temporarily invalid and may be restored to VALID. This status is represented by 0x02 in the SLT.

    As a result, the Credential Issuer MUST set the bits parameter in the SLT's status_list object to 2.

    The Credential Issuer MAY define additional application-specific statuses within the range supported by bits. It SHOULD carefully consider the information disclosed to Relying Parties when defining additional statuses for the Digital Credential lifecycle.

    The Credential Issuer MUST pack entries starting with the least significant bit of each byte, compress the byte array using DEFLATE with the ZLIB data format, and publish the resulting Status List in the SLT.

    Status List Token

    The Credential Issuer MUST act as both the Status Issuer and the Status Provider. It MUST make each SLT available via HTTP GET at the URI specified by the Digital Credential's Status List reference, using application/statuslist+jwt for a JWT SLT or application/statuslist+cwt for a CWT SLT.

    The SLT format MAY be either a JWT or a CWT and MUST be protected by a cryptographic signature. Regardless of the chosen format, the SLT MUST conform to Section 5.1 for JWTs or Section 5.2 for CWTs of the IETF Token Status List draft-21.

    Regardless of the format, the Credential Issuer MUST sign the SLT using one of the following:

    • a valid X.509 certificate whose trust chain terminates at the Trust Anchor published in the Attestation Provider's LoTE when it is registered in the EUDIW Trust Framework; or
    • a valid key attested by the Attestation Provider's Entity Configuration when it is registered in the National Trust Framework.

    10.2 Deployed Test Case Matrix¶

    • An initial code describing the track (CRL, OCSP, TSL).
    • A second code for the type of checks (REQ for request schema, RES for response schema, VAL for validation checks).
    • An increasing natural number.
